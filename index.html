How Far Has the Stock Market Fallen in the Year Before U.S. Presidential Elections?

24 U.S. presidential elections · S&P 500 daily closing prices · A one-year lookback from each Election Day. Four indicators: pre-election return, maximum drawdown, drawdown trough month, and whether the White House changed parties.

How the measurement window works:For the November 5, 2024 election, the calendar-date window is 2023/11/04—2024/11/04; if the start or end date falls on a market holiday or weekend, use the first and last available trading-day closes within that window. Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline from an earlier closing-price high to a subsequent closing-price low, not simply the difference between the highest and lowest prices regardless of order. Returns exclude dividends.

How to Read Maximum Drawdown

The year before the 2020 election finished up 7.54% even though its maximum drawdown reached -33.92%. In other words, the market suffered a sharp decline during the period but had recovered by the last pre-election close.

That is very different from a market that declines steadily from the start to the end of the window.

Data Source & Limitations

Price data are sourced from the public Macrotrends S&P 500 Daily CSV (GitHub mirror). The dataset runs through December 4, 2024, covering all election windows in this study.

Pre-1957 values are historical/back-calculated S&P series, and older Saturday trading sessions may differ in coverage. An association between stock-market performance and a change of governing party is not evidence that any party intentionally caused an economic downturn.

About this report: This page does not store personal information. It retrieves market prices from a public GitHub-hosted CSV.
